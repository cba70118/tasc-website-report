Where the website stands with search engines and AI

tascperformance.comAs of 7 October 2026Shopify store

Overall the site is in solid shape. Search engines and AI tools can find and read every page, most shoppers get pages that load quickly, and tasc holds its own on speed against other activewear brands. The weak spots are the details search engines and AI tools receive about products and blog posts, a homepage that jumps around while it loads, and pages that carry far more weight than they need.

Why the pages are heavy

The homepage downloads 13.5 MB, up from 11.5 MB in September. A healthy store page is about 2 MB. Much of that is code for add-on apps (Yotpo reviews, Klaviyo email sign-up and others) that loads before the page itself. About 2 MB of it is never even used on the homepage.

Why the page jumps around

Images on the site don't tell the browser how big they are, so the browser can't save room for them. As each image arrives, everything below it gets pushed down. Shoppers go to tap something and it moves. This is the problem real shoppers are hitting on the homepage.

The cause sits in the site's theme, so it behaves the same way wherever those images appear.

Serious

Search engines can't use the product and blog details

Behind each page, the site gives search engines and AI tools a short summary of what's on it: a product's price and star rating, or a blog post's title and author. Search engines use this to show extras in results, like review stars under a product link. Two mistakes are stopping that from working.

Blog posts
295 posts

The summary on every blog post has a formatting error, so search engines throw it away. In effect, no post is sending this information. All 14 posts checked had the problem.

Cause: a formatting error in the blog template, so every post is affected the same way.

Product pages
597 products

Every product with reviews sends two different summaries: one from the Shopify theme with all sizes and prices, and one from the Yotpo reviews app with the star rating but only one price. Search engines may pick either, or trust neither. If it picks the theme's version, ratings like 4.9 stars from 1,508 reviews won't show in search results. 16 of the 20 products checked send both versions.

Cause: the Shopify theme and the Yotpo reviews app each add their own version.

Worth knowing: resolving the product issue means settling on one of the two versions. Which one depends on how Yotpo's star ratings are set up, so it involves Yotpo as well as the site theme.

Collection pages (like Women's Yoga) send clean, correct details, so the problem is limited to the product and blog templates.

Moderate

More than half the images have no description

848 of 1,483

images checked have no written description. Screen readers used by blind shoppers skip them, and image search can't tell what they show.

The gaps show up in the same spots on every page, mostly the 16 or so small images in the menu. That points to the site's theme rather than hundreds of individual images. The share is up from 42% in September because pages now carry fewer images overall while those menu images are still unlabelled. Other accessibility gaps flagged in testing: text that's hard to read against its background, some links and buttons with no name, and tap targets too small on phones.

Working well

What's already in good shape

A lot of the setup is better than most online stores.

  • Search engines can find and read every page they should, and are kept out of cart and checkout pages.
  • All versions of the web address lead cleanly to one main address.
  • The site map lists all 994 pages, neatly organized.
  • Broken links show a proper "page not found," which many Shopify stores get wrong.
  • Company and store details (New Orleans, Birmingham, Dallas, Houston) are set up for search engines in full, with addresses and hours.
  • Collection page titles and descriptions are well written, and the strongest on the site.
  • Images below the fold wait to load until the shopper scrolls to them.
  • Basic security settings are in place.
About this check

What this report does and doesn't cover

Everything here was re-measured on the live site on 7 October 2026, except real-shopper speed data, which is from 19 September (see below). Sources: two runs of Google's Lighthouse lab test, Google's real-shopper speed data (PageSpeed Insights and CrUX Vis), and a page-by-page scan of 55 pages: 20 product pages, 14 blog posts, 8 collections, the homepage and 12 other pages.

  • Real-shopper speed data, for tasc and for competitors, is from 19 September. Google's free data feed was at its daily limit on 7 October, so it couldn't be refreshed. It covers the whole site and the homepage; individual product and collection pages weren't checked on their own.
  • We didn't have access to Google Search Console or analytics, so this doesn't cover rankings, traffic or which searches bring people in.
  • AI visibility covers what AI tools can reach and read, plus 5 roundup articles. It doesn't measure what AI assistants actually say about tasc.
  • Competitors were compared on speed only. Their search listings, links from other websites and keyword opportunities weren't reviewed.

Not covered yet: rankings, traffic and sales impact would need Google Search Console and analytics access. A scan of all 994 pages would confirm how widely the template problems reach.
